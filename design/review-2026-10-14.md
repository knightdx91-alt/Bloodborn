# Marrowmark — where it actually stands, 2026-10-14

A second look at the game, the plan, and what the project has learnt.
Written to be read on a phone: short sections, no wide tables. The
first feasibility pass (`feasibility-review.md`, 2026-07-28) judged
the design. This one judges the project — the thing that exists in the
repository today, against the plan it says it is following.

Everything below was checked in a fresh container on the day, not
read off `STATUS.md`. Where the two disagree, this file says so.

---

## 1. The verdict, in five lines

1. **The combat core is real and the design around it is unusually
   coherent.** Stage 1 of `tech.md` §6 is genuinely done, the sim has
   408 passing tests, and the one measurement that could have killed
   the combat design (`spar.gd`) came out in its favour.
2. **The project has drifted from its own order of work.** It says
   "build the thing that can say no, first". The thing that can say
   no is Stage 2 — two clients, a server, a latency slider and a
   person's verdict on feel. Roughly twenty-seven of the last fifty-two commits
   went to procedural country instead, which cannot say no to anything
   the design depends on.
3. **The world is now sized for a studio, not for one person.** The
   ×15 growth to 3,870 km² was decided from "3× Skyrim by feel" and
   never re-run against the feasibility review's population maths or
   the solo content strategy. It should be re-argued, not quietly kept.
4. **The handoff is no longer a handoff.** `STATUS.md` is 3,062 lines
   and 153 KB, contradicts itself on what is finished, and reports a
   test count that is 73 short. The browser build the README points at
   is a month and 36 commits stale.
5. **Nothing has moved for seventeen days.** `tech.md` §6 names
   abandonment as the single largest risk. The next session should be
   one that ends with a controller in a hand, not a faster chunk.

## 2. What is actually there (measured today)

- **Run today, in a fresh container:** `dotnet test` (408 pass),
  `worldcheck` (all clear), `qacheck` (all clear), and `facingshot`
  renders the player on the north road with the mill and the Hedges
  sign in frame. The build on `main` is green on everything I could
  reach.
- **`sim/`:** 408 tests, all passing, 166 ms. The Combat, Items,
  Crafting, Progression, Town, Net and World namespaces are all
  present. This is the best-tested part of the project by a distance.
- **`prototype/`:** ~12,400 lines of GDScript, of which ~5,600 are
  check harnesses (22 of them) and ~8,800 are the world, town, NPC and
  rules subsystems. One scene, Thornfield, with the drill yard and
  the Hedges reachable on foot, and a separate "spike" scene for the
  generated world.
- **CI:** the Android APK builds green on every push to `main`.
  **Nothing else runs in CI.** The 408 C# tests, the tuning-drift
  guard (`TuningFileTests`) and the 22 harnesses are all run by hand
  in the assistant's container. The guard that "fails the build if the
  numbers drift" only fails a build somebody chooses to run.
- **The browser build** (`docs/`) was last exported on 2026-09-16.
  `prototype/` has changed in 36 commits since, including the
  one-world merge, the stamina amendments, the sprint fix and the
  generated country. The README's "Play the prototype" link serves the
  old launcher with two separate scenes.
- **Commit cadence:** 52 commits total; 35 on 2026-09-27 alone, one
  on 2026-10-09, none since.
- **Assets:** 319 MB in `prototype/assets`, all plain git objects
  (LFS was never active — fixed in the last commit). Roughly 911 of
  those models are the KayKit packs the game cannot use.

## 3. The game as it plays

**What is strong.**

- The three-answer combat loop exists, is wired to one shared stamina
  bar, and has been played with a pad. Parry refunds, failed parry
  costs more than not trying, committed attacks are unparryable,
  exhaustion is visible on the body, and an empty bar refuses the
  dodge. Every one of those came out of play and went through `sim/`
  first. That discipline is the project's real asset.
- The town loop closes: take a cull off a board that is generated from
  world state, walk north, kill boars, walk back, hand in, get paid,
  and the next posting is smaller because the pressure dropped. Topics
  resolve against the world (L92), the confirm gate cannot be stepped
  around, and the market board refuses to lie about empty stock.
- The input layer is further along than most hobby projects ever get:
  pad, touch and keyboard detected from use, safe-area insets, viewport
  scaling, a focus trap on the gate, and a harness for each.

**What is missing, and matters.**

- **The arcs are invisible.** Overhead, level and low cuts all play the
  same clip. L64/L65 say the player learns where they are aiming by
  watching where the blow lands, so a directional system nobody can
  see is a directional system nobody can learn. This is blocked on
  four Mixamo clips (`SPEC-attack-clips.md`), has been the "highest
  value blocked item" since 2026-09-14, and the block is external. It
  needs an hour on mixamo.com, not another session of engine work.
- **Heavy and committed share a clip.** `combat.md` §6 calls animation
  readability a hard requirement. Right now they are told apart by
  timing, which is the thing §6 says must not be the way.
- **Night is a guess.** L89 flagged "how dark before §6 breaks" as
  open. It is lit by a moon now, and nobody has fought at night on a
  real screen.
- **The generated country is country and nothing else.** No boars, no
  hamlet NPCs, no contracts, no delve mouths. The town has a hole cut
  for it in the terrain and the road runs 23 km to a capitol that is
  a coordinate. You can walk for six and a half hours and nothing
  happens, which is the opposite of what `tech.md` §1a promised
  ("hand-place only what players will remember").
- **The §9 gate is not what Stage 1 tested.** `combat.md` §9 wants
  three enemies, six weapon families, two techniques and a latency
  slider. Stage 1 delivered one enemy with three shapes, one sword, no
  techniques and no slider. Stage 1 being "complete" is true of
  `tech.md`'s six steps and not of the gate they serve. Both documents
  should say so in one place.

## 4. The plan, evaluated

### 4.1 The design side is complete — and that is a strength

Ninety-three locked decisions, every pillar closed or reduced to a
prototype question, nineteen documents, and the locks cross-reference
each other by number. The feasibility review's verdict stands: the
loops close, the exploit surface is pre-answered, and the grief
resistance is real. The design is better than the typical solo MMO
design by a wide margin and should not be reopened.

Two caveats. The feasibility review predates L40–L93 and says so; a
second pass is owed and has been owed since 2026-09-08. And "design
complete" has quietly become permission to stop making design calls:
the world-size decision (below) was a structural decision made in a
session log rather than as a lock, and it is the largest one since L54.

### 4.2 The order of work has inverted

`tech.md` §6 and `combat.md` §9 are explicit: L39 is the one result
that can kill the project, Stage 2 is where it is passed or failed,
and it "cannot be attempted before Stage 1 exists". Stage 1 has existed
since 2026-09-27.

What happened next was roughly twenty-seven commits of terrain,
rivers, roads, bridges, bakes, fingerprints and chunk profiling. All of it good work,
all of it measured, none of it on the critical path. The justification
given was "the spike can say no to the phone". But the phone is a test
rig, not a ship target (L15, L51, L53). A 2017-class phone failing to
stream 3,870 km² would change how the prototype is played, not what
the game is.

Stage 2's half that needs no human is already done: `sim/Net/` proves a
perfect parry lands identically at 0 and 200 ms. The half that needs a
human — two clients, a slider, a person saying "I cannot tell" — has
not been started, and is the only thing left that can end the project.
**It should be next.**

### 4.3 The world is sized against the wrong constraint

The ×15 growth was argued from one input: a crossing should take three
times as long as walking across Skyrim. That is a legitimate feel
target. Three things were not put against it:

- **Population.** `feasibility-review.md` §3.1 treats 1–2k concurrent
  as the floor and says to size the Wheel to feel full at that floor.
  At 3,870 km² and 2,000 players, that is one player per two square
  kilometres. Thin markets are the design's own named failure mode,
  and distance is what makes them thin.
- **Content.** `tech.md` §1 says a solo developer wins on systems and
  buys volume. Country is cheap; places are not. Six towns, each with
  40–80 conversational NPCs across the whole world, a delve ecology and
  hand-composed fighting ground do not scale with area, so the extra
  3,600 km² is either empty or authored by hand. The log admits this
  ("ten times that problem") and keeps the size anyway.
- **The mount.** The 2.5× mount is doing all the work in making a
  130-minute spoke tolerable, and no mount exists.

A 255 km² world with a 22-minute spoke was derived from a design
commitment (the ambush window). The 3,870 km² world was derived from a
comparison. The first derivation was the better one. **Recommendation:
re-argue it as a lock, with population density as an input, and
expect to land between the two** — a 50-minute spoke, not 20 and not
130. The generator takes one constant (`WORLD_SCALE`), so this costs
nothing to change and everything to leave unexamined.

### 4.4 Things the plan has right

- **Buy the art, author the fighting** (L85). Correct, and the
  evidence renders show it working: bought kits unify under one
  lighting pass, and the log's own placeholder list is the hand-made
  things (the boar from primitives, the blocky NPC bodies).
- **`sim/` as the authority, GDScript as a mirror with a named exit**
  (L88). The port of the town rules found three real bugs, which is
  the argument for it made concrete. The exit condition (native
  builds) is the right one and a Windows PC remains the right buy.
- **Contract boards over quest chains** (L29). The cheapest content
  model in the design and already the best-working thing in the town.
- **The spike discipline** — build the thing that can say no first.
  Right rule, applied to the wrong question in September.

### 4.5 Open risks the plan is not carrying

- **P11's inference cost vs. buy-to-play (L27).** Deferred until the
  slice measures it. Fine, but every town NPC built today is authored
  text, so no number is getting closer. If the answer turns out to be
  "subscription", that changes the pitch, the store page and the
  moderation posture, and it is cheaper to know in year one.
- **Trademark clearance** blocks anything public and has not moved.
  It also gates the real signing key. A clearance search is a few
  hundred dollars and an afternoon, and it unblocks the first thing
  the plan cannot do from a container.
- **No CI for the rules.** The tuning-drift guard and 408 tests exist
  to catch exactly the bug class the project has hit five times
  (a rule living in one place and not the other). They run when
  remembered. A fifteen-line workflow fixes this permanently.
- **One contributor's memory is the only index.** `STATUS.md` has
  become a journal. The parts a new session needs — what is built,
  what is next, what is blocked, what the numbers are — are in there,
  but under 150 KB of history, and the headline section still says
  step 4 is unjudged while the "Next" section says it is done.

## 5. What the project has figured out

These are the findings worth carrying forward whatever else changes.
Most of them are in `STATUS.md`; this is the short list.

- **The three answers hold up under measurement.** Dodging *around*
  makes ten times the openings of dodging *away*, parry is the
  high-reward answer, and no answer is strictly dominant. L56 is
  vindicated by numbers, not just by argument.
- **A metric without the goal in it ranks the strategies that ignore
  the goal first.** The dodge was "dominant" until the bot was asked to
  win. This generalises to every tuning instrument the project builds.
- **Past the latency clamp it is a cliff, not a slope.** `sim/Net/`
  found that §7 as written gives no band where some blows land and
  some do not. Three fixes are written up in `sim/NETWORKING.md`; the
  partial-credit one is the only one that produces a slope and is
  already a concept in §1b. This is a design decision waiting to be
  made and it should be made before Stage 2 is built, not during.
- **Look at the frame before averaging over it.** Lighting, the
  camera jerk, the black world, the one-colour hamlet, the paper
  bridge, the waist-deep townsfolk: six times a render caught what
  arithmetic passed. The harnesses now render on purpose.
- **A check that has never failed is not a check.** Mutation-testing
  every new assertion is now routine and has caught "could not fail"
  checks at least seven times. Keep doing it.
- **Rules that trust their caller are the bug class.** Take, hire, the
  market, the exhausted dodge, the walk speed in town — all the same
  shape. The gate belongs in the rule, which is the real argument for
  `sim/` being the authority.
- **Topics answer to the world** (L92). The one design rule that came
  *from* the prototype rather than to it, and the most reusable thing
  the town produced.
- **Performance theories were wrong five times out of five**, and the
  measurement was right every time. Profile, then cut.

## 6. What to do next, in order

Each item ends with something that can be put in front of a person.

1. **Get the four clips.** `SPEC-attack-clips.md`, direct from Mixamo,
   no Blender. It is the only item that has been top of the blocked
   list for a month and needs a browser, not a build. The assistant
   wires them the same day.
2. **Republish the browser build and add a test workflow.** Both are
   container work. The README should point at the game that exists,
   and `dotnet test` should run on every push beside the APK.
3. **Rewrite `STATUS.md` as a handoff** — a one-screen "where things
   stand" with the real numbers, the blocked list and the next three
   items — and move the journal to `docs/journal/` by date. Keep every
   word; change where it lives.
4. **Decide the latency slope** (`sim/NETWORKING.md`'s three options)
   and lock it. A phone session's worth of work, and it has to precede
   Stage 2.
5. **Stage 2, step 7: two clients, one server, a latency slider**, in
   the drill yard, with the one enemy. The server runs `sim/Net/`
   unchanged, which is the bet `tech.md` makes. End with a build where
   the slider is on screen and the developer can play against the
   enemy at 0 and 100 ms on the same evening. That is the L39 verdict.
6. **Re-lock the world size** with population density as an input.
   One constant changes. Do it before another hour is spent on
   generating country at the current scale.
7. **Only then, join the country to the game**: boars in the Hedges
   cell, a hamlet board, one delve mouth. The generator is ready for
   this and nothing in it needs to be faster first.

What the phone can do from here: 1 (an hour on mixamo.com), 4, 6, and
the decisions inside 3. The rest is container work the assistant can
do in any session.
