# Bloodborn — Lore (working canon v1)

STATUS: **WORKING CANON** (adopted 2026-07-26). Names remain open to
polish, but the structures below are the basis for content design.
Tone dials (locked): M-rated grounded dark, low-magic medieval.

---

## 1. The name

**The Bloodborn are the awakened — and the world does not know they
exist.** Long ago a god died at the center of the world. Its blood
soaked into the land — and in some people, the blood *quickens*. Those
few are called Bloodborn, but only by each other, in whispers. To
everyone else, mages are a fireside story: the witch in the wood, the
saint who never was, the man who spoke fire in your grandmother's
grandmother's day. **Magic is myth.**

The crucial line: the world *does* know the blood works wonders — the
dying knitted back at shrines, the saints' blessings, the rites of the
Church. But those are **miracles**: institutional, sanctioned, the
god's doing. That a *person* could wield the blood — that is
superstition, heresy-tinged nonsense, a thing no serious person
believes. The awakened hide inside that disbelief.

The game is named after what the player might secretly become. Players
don't learn this at character creation. They learn it the first time
they see a stranger close a wound with a word — or the first time they
do it themselves — and realize the fireside story is true.

## 2. Cosmology — why the world is a Wheel

At the center of the world is the **Godsgrave**: the place where the
god fell. Nobody agrees on what killed it, or whether "killed" is even
the right word — the faiths exist because of this disagreement (§4).

- The **capitol** was built in rings around the grave site over
  centuries of pilgrimage. It is the largest city in the world because
  every road in the world was walked toward it first.
- The **six great roads** (the spokes) are the old processional ways —
  six ancient pilgrim routes, now trade arteries.
- The **six towns** grew where the pilgrim roads crossed good land:
  ore, pasture, water, timber. Wheel by history, not by design.
- The god's blood seeped outward unevenly. Each **wedge** is
  blood-touched differently — which is why the iron of one region
  takes an edge no other iron takes, why the beasts of another grow
  strange and large, why certain fens glow on new-moon nights. (This
  is the lore engine behind regional crafting materials, L4.)
- Monsters are **blood-warped**: ordinary creatures changed by pooled
  blood in deep places. The deeper the pooling, the worse what walks
  out. The wedges are dangerous because the blood is.

## 3. Death, and why it works the way it does

The god's blood in the land does not let people go easily. The dying
are *knitted back* — dragged to their feet at the nearest shrine,
gasping, poorer, but alive. Ordinary death (L17) is survivable because
**the blood refuses your death**. Every commoner knows this; nobody
finds it strange; it is simply how the world has always worked — and
crucially, nobody calls it *magic*. It is the god's grace, a miracle,
weather. The public wonder of the world is entirely institutional and
ambient; none of it suggests a person could ever hold the reins.

Beyond the Godsgrave, it is different. Cross into the god's own domain
and the blood is no longer a tide that carries you back — it is the
sea you drown in. **There, the dead stay dead** (L12). This is the
cosmological floor under the death ladder: the same blood explains
respawning, wedge danger, awakening, and permadeath.

## 4. The faiths (proposal: four)

All four worship the same dead god. They cannot agree on what its
death *was* — and each faith's answer shapes what its followers do.
Faction rep (L12) is standing within one of these. Their cathedrals
face each other across the Godsgrave in the capitol's cathedral
quarter, and their gates are the six ascension doors.

Because magic is myth, no faith has a *public* position on mages —
there is nothing, officially, to have a position on. What they have
are public faces and inner truths:

1. **The Church of the Silent Heart** — *"The god chose to die."*
   Death as the final gift; the blood as sacrament. The orthodox,
   institutional power: cathedrals, tithes, saints, certification of
   shrines. Canonization of fallen demigods (L13) is *their* rite.
   **Public face:** claims of human sorcery are peasant superstition,
   and repeating them is mild blasphemy — miracles belong to the god
   alone. **Inner truth:** a small, very old bureau — the **Office of
   the Lamp** — knows the quickening is real, and has spent centuries
   making sure the awakened are quietly found, "consecrated," and
   never heard from again. The myth is not an accident. It is the
   Office's masterpiece, maintained by erasure.
2. **The Red Vigil** — *"The god was murdered."* And whatever did it
   is still out there. A militant order: watchtowers on the rim roads,
   martial training, monster-culling as holy work. **Public face:**
   soldiers and monster-hunters; no patience for fireside tales.
   **Inner truth:** the Vigil's high circle keeps sealed records of
   "weapons that breathe" — awakened found by the Vigil vanish from
   public life and reappear as unregistered assets. Not doctrine.
   Preparation.
3. **The Gleaners** — *"The god is not dead — it dreams."* The folk
   faith of the wedges and farms: omen-reading, first-fruits
   offerings, tending the land as tending the sleeper. **The faith
   that doesn't know what it knows:** some hedge-rites *work* — a
   charm that truly staunches, a ward that truly holds — and the
   Gleaners call it luck, or the sleeper's kindness, and ask no
   further. The closest thing the awakened have to a natural
   camouflage: in Gleaner country, a small wonder is just Tuesday.
4. **The Open Vein** — *"The blood belongs to everyone."* **Public
   face:** a fringe folk movement of blood-mystics — communion
   bleedings, ecstatic rites, tolerated as harmless cranks and
   harassed when convenient. Everyone has heard their creed; everyone
   takes it for metaphor. **Inner truth: it is not metaphor.** The
   Vein's inner circle is the closest thing to a network the awakened
   have — they watch for the signs, reach the newly quickened before
   the Lamp does, and initiate them into the hidden life. Publicly
   ridiculous, secretly indispensable — the ridicule is cover they
   cultivate on purpose.

Design note: the faiths give the magic secret (L7) its social
texture. The world doesn't have opinions about mages — it has
opinions about *stories* about mages, and three secret institutions
racing each other to the newly awakened.

## 5. The six towns (names are placeholders)

| Town | Character | Wedge | Feeds the economy |
|------|-----------|-------|-------------------|
| **Hammarsted** | Forge town: soot, guild-pride, the best steel in the world | The Ironbarrens — scarred ore hills | Smithing capital; blood-touched iron veins |
| **Vellmark** | Horse town: studs, drovers, horse-lords' feuds | The Reaches — open grass plains | Mounts, cavalry, caravan stock; mounted-combat culture (L-war) |
| **Greywater** | River-port: barges, smugglers, fish-stink and money | The Fens — waterways and drowned meadows | Trade hub, shipwrights, the grey market |
| **Thornfield** | Harvest town: granaries, breweries, market fairs | The Hedges — farmland into old orchard-wood | Food, cloth, brewing; the "safe" starting feel that makes the rest darker |
| **Candlerow** | Scribes' town: chandlers, archives, bell-towers, quiet | The Wistwood — old forest with older ruins | Ink, paper, lore; awakening anomalies cluster here — nobody says so aloud |
| **Coldharrow** | Hunters' town: tanneries, palisades, hard people | The Marches — dark pine highlands | Furs, leather, monster-parts; the dangerous frontier wedge |

(Candlerow replaces the earlier "mage town" sketch — an overt mage
town would spoil the magic secret. It reads as a dull scholars' town
until the player learns what the archives are actually about.)

## 6. The capitol — Godsgrave

Extra-large hub (L1), ringed districts:

- **The Grave itself** at the center: a walled, guarded... something.
  The faiths jointly control access. This is the **ascension gate** —
  and until a player is near max rep and level, all they know is that
  people who go in do not come out.
- **The Cathedral Quarter** — four great churches glaring at each
  other across the pilgrim square.
- **The Great Ring** — the arena (L6): fight nights, beast bouts, and
  the betting houses around it. **Echo bouts** are staged here too —
  publicly framed as Church rite-spectacle (the faiths raising a
  saint's shade is a *miracle*, sanctioned and awesome; it implies
  nothing about living mages).
- **The Veiled Ring** — the Great Ring after dark, unofficially. The
  hidden fight circuit where the awakened duel, where underground
  books take bets from those who know, and where **Succession Trials**
  (L10) convene. Mechanics unchanged from L10 — open free-for-all
  among qualified grandmasters, any combat goes — but the audience is
  whoever knows to come, and the authorities' official position is
  that no such gathering occurs. (Adjusts the earlier assumption that
  Trials were public spectacle; the myth requires the veil. As player
  mages multiply on a server, how loud these nights get is emergent.)
- **The Concourse** — grand market, auction houses, banks, the
  property market's high end.
- **The Monument** (L13) — a colonnade of black stone in the pilgrim
  square. Every demigod who ever lived and died, carved. Spell
  founders too (§5.3-brainstorm). Tourists read it. Children dare each
  other to touch it.

## 7. The demigod realm (sketch — P10 still open)

Beyond the Grave is the god's dying interior — landscape as wound:
plains of slow-pulsing red, rivers that remember being veins, weather
that follows grief. Those who enter take on a fraction of godhood
(demigod, L12) and are unmade permanently if slain (L14). The faiths
call those who enter **the Interred**. What the realm's areas are, and
what waits at the deepest layer, is P10's conversation — but the
proposal is: advancement inward = descending *toward the heart*, and
whatever is at the heart is the game's final mystery.

## 8. Resolved lore decisions (2026-07-26)

- **Names:** placeholders adopted as working names. Rename freely
  before any public material; content design proceeds with these.
- **Anyone is awakenable.** No chosen bloodline. The blood is in the
  land and in everyone; awakening is a matter of experience, not
  birthright. (This is also the Open Vein's whole argument — and it
  happens to be true.) Mechanically: every character can find magic;
  none is promised it.
- **Magic is publicly a myth (DECIDED — supersedes the earlier
  "legality varies" framing).** There is no law against sorcery,
  because officially sorcery does not exist. That absence is the
  threat:
  - An exposed mage gets **no trial**. A crowd that sees the
    impossible doesn't think "wizard" — it thinks **blood-warped**,
    the same word they use for the monsters. Fear, mob, fire.
  - The institutions that know better move *quietly*: the Office of
    the Lamp erases, the Vigil's high circle collects, the Open
    Vein's inner circle rescues. Three secret hands racing to every
    newly quickened — none of them advertising.
  - In Gleaner country, small wonders pass unremarked — natural
    camouflage, but no protection if something big is witnessed.
  - Early-mage gameplay is therefore *secrecy under pressure*: don't
    be seen; if seen, control the story; if the story escapes, decide
    which hidden hand finds you first.

## 9. Secret canon — what actually happened (SPOILERS, internal only)

Decided now so content can be written coherently. Never stated
outright in-game before the deepest P10 content.

**All four faiths are right.**

The god *chose* to die (Silent Heart) — because something was coming
that it could not defeat awake (Red Vigil: there *is* an enemy, and
the Vigil's paranoia is justified). Its death was not an ending but a
transformation: the body became a seal, the blood a living ward
soaked into the world (Gleaners: it dreams — the heart still beats at
the bottom of the Godsgrave). And a seal made of blood needs blood to
hold: the quickening is the god's ongoing recruitment (Open Vein: the
blood really does belong to everyone).

Implications:
- The demigod realm is the inside of the seal. Advancing inward =
  descending toward the **Heart**. Every demigod's permadeath *feeds
  the seal* — "when you die you get something" runs both ways: the
  world gets something too. The Interred are, unknowingly, the ward's
  maintenance.
- What waits at the Heart, what the enemy is, and what a player-god
  in the pantheon actually does to the seal — reserved for P10.
  Expansion hook: the seal weakening.
