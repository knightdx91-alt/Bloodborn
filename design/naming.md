# Naming — the title problem

Raised 2026-09-08. **"Bloodborn" cannot ship as the title.**

---

## 1. Why it has to change

**The legal reason.** *Bloodborne* (FromSoftware / Sony, 2015) is a
live, well-known trademark registered in exactly the goods class this
project occupies. "Bloodborn" is one silent letter away and
**phonetically identical** — which is the test that matters, since
trademark law turns on likelihood of confusion, not spelling. A dark
fantasy action game called Bloodborn is close to the worst case: same
genre, same tone, same audience, near-identical mark. This is not a
risk to manage, it is a position with no defence.

**The design reason, which is independent and just as strong.** L36
mandates **total marketing silence on magic** — the game ships as a
grounded crafting-and-war MMO, and the leak is the marketing. A title
containing "blood" and "born" promises the supernatural on the box. It
argues against the game's own best secret before a player has read a
word. The right title should sound like a game about trade routes,
wars, and a hard country — and only look like foreshadowing in
hindsight, once players know what the grave at the centre is.

## 2. Criteria

1. **No hint of magic or the supernatural** (L36). This rules out the
   whole *quicken / blood / soul / arcane* family, however tempting.
2. **Evokes the real premise:** the grave at the centre, the wheel of
   towns around it, the roads between, the things made by hand.
3. **Coined or compound, not descriptive.** Distinctive marks are both
   easier to register and easier to search for. Generic words are
   nearly impossible to protect and invisible in a storefront.
4. **Reads M-rated grounded dark** (L20) — weight and plainness, not
   ornament.
5. **Searchable.** If the first page of results is something else, the
   name has already failed.

## 3. Shortlist

| Candidate | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| **Wheelgrave** | Names both structural facts of the world at once — the Wheel (L1) and the god's grave. Coined, so highly distinctive and registrable. Sounds like a place, not a power fantasy. No conflicting game found. **Current lead.** | Slightly opaque on first read; "grave" does some tonal work that a player only cashes in later |
| **Marrowmark** | Carries the maker's-mark idea (L4) and the body-of-the-god imagery. Coined, distinctive, good mouthfeel | "Marrow" leans faintly supernatural; shares a syllable with the Interior's Marrow ring, which may confuse internal language |
| **Coldmarch** | Grounded, martial, distance-forward — very on-theme for L28's "distance is real". No magic hint whatsoever | Says nothing about the grave or the making; could be any medieval war game |
| **Sixtowns** | Plain and confident; names the Wheel without explaining it. Very easy to say and search | Reads a little small and cosy for M-rated dark |
| **The Long Wheel** | Evocative and slightly strange; article-led titles age well | Three words, weaker as a logo, and "wheel" alone is heavily used |

**Recommendation: Wheelgrave**, pending real clearance. It is the only
candidate that encodes both halves of the premise, it is a coined
compound (the strongest position for registration), and it keeps the
secret — a player reads "grave" as a place name until the day they
learn something is buried there.

## 4. The in-world term needs the same treatment

L21 uses **"Bloodborn" as the in-world name for the awakened.** Even
with the title changed, keeping that as prominent in-game vocabulary
re-creates a smaller version of the same problem, and there is a
better word sitting in the lore already: `lore.md` says the god's
blood *quickens* in people.

**Proposed: "the Quickened."** It grows out of the existing canon
rather than being imported, it is period-plausible as folk vocabulary,
and it carries the right sense of something happening *to* a person
rather than something they were born as — which is also more accurate,
since L21 establishes that anyone is awakenable.

## 5. What clearance actually requires

**The searches behind this document are not clearance.** They rule out
the obvious, nothing more. Before the name goes on anything public:

- A trademark search in the relevant classes — **Nice class 9**
  (software) and **class 41** (entertainment services) — across USPTO,
  EUIPO, and UKIPO at minimum.
- A **common-law** search: unregistered use by indie games,
  tabletop titles, novels, and bands can still create a conflict.
- Domain and storefront availability, plus the major social handles.
- **A trademark attorney.** This is the one line item on the entire
  project that a solo developer should not do themselves. It is
  comparatively cheap, and the failure mode — rebranding after launch,
  or a takedown — is not.

Do this **before** the vertical slice goes anywhere public, and before
any store page, domain, or social account exists. Renaming a project
is trivial today and expensive the day after the first trailer.
